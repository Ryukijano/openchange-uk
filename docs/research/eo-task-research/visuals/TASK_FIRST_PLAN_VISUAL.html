The plan in one line

Start with coastal change. The thing worth learning is how wrong each satellite shoreline is, checked against field surveys. Forecasting comes second. Burn-scar search needs no trained model.

  1. 1. Coastal: is the change real? Train a small model on top of existing tools (CoastSat, CoastSeg) that predicts each image's shoreline error and says when to abstain.
  2. 2. Greenness forecast Fine-tune Contextformer with radar input, but only if a cheap CPU test shows radar helps. Licence (CC BY-NC-SA) still to decide.
  3. 3. Burn-scar analogues No training. Shape and severity measurements plus nearest-neighbour search. Labels from MTBS would make a trained model circular.

Why coastal: the waterline moves even when the beach doesn't

A satellite sees where the water is at that moment. A survey measures a fixed height on the beach. Move the tide and see how far apart they get.

What else makes one image wrong

  • Tide and wavesWater sits higher or lower on the beach than the survey height.
  • Image shiftedImages are not perfectly aligned to the ground. The RSMA paper found this part does not go away with contour tuning.
  • Clouds and shadowsPartly hidden coast or a bad water/sand threshold.
  • Method disagreementThe 5 SDS_Benchmark teams' lines spread apart on hard images. The spread is a useful warning sign.

What the two overlap papers changed

Neither kills its idea. Both narrow what we can claim as new.

Coastal: Aladejana et al. 2026

Regional Studies in Marine Science, doi:10.1016/j.rsma.2026.105374. Abstract read; full text is closed.

  • They already ask our question, but per beach and per decade, using a physical noise budget.
  • From the abstract: no learned model, no beaches without surveys, no per-image error.
Change: our claim becomes "predict each image's error, and transfer that to beaches with no surveys." Their noise budget is added as baseline 9.

Forecasting: Defonte et al. 2026

arXiv 2605.04239 (Thales, CNES). Full text read.

Change: "radar helps" is no longer new in general. Ours must be forecast-only, per horizon (5–30 days), against persistence and climatology, on African rangeland. Their small gain also lowers our odds of passing K1.

The other two ideas, in pictures

Forecast greenness 5–30 days ahead, with radar filling cloud gaps

Clouds hide many optical images. Radar sees through clouds. Does radar history make the forecast better than just carrying the last value forward, or using the usual value for that time of year?

Find past fires that looked like this one, or say there are none

Give a burn footprint and severity map. Get back verified past US fires with a similar shape and severity, from MTBS. Answering "no close match" correctly matters as much as finding one.
