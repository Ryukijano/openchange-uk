# Coastal model method / novelty red team

**Evidence checked: 13 October 2026. Research only: no imagery or weights downloaded, training, cluster access, commits, publication or deployment.**

## Verdict

Build an **observation-reliability and coastal-change measurement system**, then test whether coastal continued pretraining helps it. Do not start by training a new foundation model. Existing encoders already cover multispectral, temporal and multisensor representations; coastal segmentation, coastal continual pretraining, learned QC, physical detectability and pixel confidence all have prior art.

The useful question is: **at an unseen coast, can we distinguish a genuine datum-referenced shoreline displacement from extraction, registration and water-level error, with useful abstention and low-label transfer?** Better segmentation alone does not answer it. A head on four sandy beaches is a pilot, not a coastal foundation model. An LLM, RLHF or preference-optimisation objective is unnecessary.

Facts below are linked to primary papers, author repositories or model cards. Proposed targets, architectures and thresholds are marked as proposals. This is a bounded targeted review, not an exhaustive novelty search. The literature-triage skill's 15–30-paper/full-read workflow was reduced to the requested model shortlist and directly relevant prior art; no claim that the literature is exhausted.

## 2. Prior art that invalidates easy novelty claims

- **NatShore is a processing framework, not a pretrained coastal encoder.** [Author repository](https://github.com/isaacgbhk/NatShore), MIT; cited paper [10.1016/j.jag.2026.105277](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2026.105277). It already combines physics-informed spatial units, tide-aware image selection, Landsat/S2 cross-mission processing, MACWE segmentation and pixel confidence. Repository evidence does not prove calibrated per-observation survey-error transfer; neither does it justify saying “no uncertainty exists.”
- **Coastal continued pretraining plus PEFT already exists.** Ma et al., [10.1109/TGRS.2025.3573159](https://doi.org/10.1109/TGRS.2025.3573159), [author institution abstract](https://pure.ecnu.edu.cn/en/publications/parameter-efficient-fine-tuning-with-frequency-adapter-for-enhanc/), describe coastal SSL continual pretraining, Freq-Adapter and a lightweight detail head for sea–land segmentation. Abstract verified; exact dataset/split/scores, code and checkpoint licence not established here. “First coastal adapter/continual pretraining” is not defensible.
- **Prithvi shoreline transfer already exists.** [Chhabra et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10177v1) use **225 S2 images / two Maldivian islands**, train/validation/test **181/22/22**, frozen ViT backbone, 30-epoch head training. With five labelled training images, their table reports IoU **0.8509 (300M)** and **0.7977 (600M)**. This is not a documented unseen-island survey-metre test; F1/IoU aggregation is not specified sufficiently to reinterpret as datum error. Dataset's public GitHub link is not a licence: no standard root LICENSE found at the checked URL. Audit before using/releasing it.
- **Coastal foundation-model applications are broader than shoreline extraction.** [Shao et al. 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/14/20/9431) address coastal land-cover classification and conversion to land-use semantics. [Jacome et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/20964471.2026.2726004) compare EO models on **85 annual S2 composite patches / 17 nations**, reporting DOFA **0.95 mIoU but 0.46 boundary-IoU**. These are habitat/semantic mapping results, not instantaneous waterline error or survey-calibrated change. Neither a model title nor high region IoU establishes the measurement capability we need. Dataset/checkpoint reuse rights were not audited here.
- **Learned QC already exists:** CoastSeg's [ImageRGBClassifier](https://huggingface.co/2320sharon/ImageRGBClassifier) and [ShorelineFilter](https://huggingface.co/2320sharon/ShorelineFilter) classify imagery and segmentations as good/bad. They are baselines, not calibrated survey-error labels. Keep CoastSat and conventional spectral/subpixel methods too.
- **The measurement gap is real but conditional.** [Vos et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-023-01001-2): five methods at four sandy beaches, roughly **10m** horizontal error at microtidal sites and **>20m** at Truc Vert. Truc Vert's lowest listed error STD is **20.1m**, versus **25.2m** for CoastSat. Different tidal screening rules and annual versus per-image outputs must be matched before learning method errors.
- Parent's abstract-only source, [RSMA 2026 105374](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rsma.2026.105374), already addresses physical site-level detectability. Include its noise-floor baseline; do not claim per-observation learned calibration is established or absent from the full paper without access. This source was supplied by the parent, not independently full-text verified here.

**Defensible candidate contribution:** jointly evaluated observation-specific error prediction, selective change reporting and low-label transfer across unseen coastal regimes. This is a hypothesis about a useful gap, not a verified “first.” Do not rename a segmentation model as a foundational coastal measurement model.

## 3. Task → gap → targets → objectives → architecture (proposal)

| Task | Target and independent validation | Primary metric |
|---|---|---|
| Extraction | Instantaneous waterline / explicitly named shoreline proxy from independent image annotation; then separately datum-referenced survey position | Boundary distance and transect bias/RMSE in metres; IoU secondary |
| QC | Expert unusable reasons (cloud, shadow, foam, registration, mixed proxy), AND survey exceedance label at a preregistered tolerance | Tail-error detection, Brier score, risk–coverage; unusable ≠ simply large erosion |
| Observation error | Signed residual of a specified method's **datum-corrected** estimate against matched field contour; error distribution/quantiles | Bias, MAE/RMSE, 95th percentile absolute error; calibrated interval width/coverage |
| Change | Signed difference at fixed datum/transect over an explicitly supported interval | Displacement error, true-event recall, false confident change reports |
| Proxy identification | Waterline, wet/dry boundary, vegetation edge, structures; separate independent expert labels | Per-proxy precision/recall, confusion and OOD rejection |

A four-site survey pilot supports error/change experiments, **not all these labels**. Survey matching lag, interpolation, alongshore mismatch and survey uncertainty belong in the target specification. Survey residual is total disagreement, not automatically pure model error. Five-algorithm consensus can be a feature/weak teacher; it is not ground truth. Annual composites cannot become per-observation labels. Preserve negative results when imagery adds nothing beyond metadata.

**First architecture:** existing EO encoder, dense shallow boundary decoder only if extraction is the measured bottleneck; transect/shoreline-strip pooled features plus a small error/QC head. Concatenate real acquisition metadata (sensor, GSD, timestamp, tide uncertainty, slope uncertainty, wave context, stable-land registration residual, missingness flags). Use separate observation-quality and morphology pathways/heads, rather than forcing one representation to be invariant to everything. A temporal pair head is optional after single-observation reliability works. No new transformer or generative world model is required initially; no architecture is claimed optimal.

Physics conditioning: use a datum correction based on tide / beach slope with documented sign, vertical datum and uncertainty; condition error on waves/runup, not a hard universal correction. Slope near zero makes corrections unstable. Coarse hindcast waves are context, not local runup measurements. Do not add independent variances in quadrature without checking covariance; survey datum and tide errors may correlate. Abstain when inputs cannot support the correction.

## 4. Pretraining objectives: useful signal versus erased dynamics (proposal)

| Candidate | Why test it | Main failure / countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Coastal masked reconstruction | Retain fine spectral mixtures, foreshore boundaries and local texture; lowest-risk continuation of a MAE encoder | Learns cloud/foam or mostly open ocean; weight valid observations, balance shoreline-strip and surrounding context, inspect boundary probes. Do not mask so aggressively that narrow coast is never visible. |
| Spatial/local masked latent prediction (JEPA optional) | Reduce redundant radiometry while keeping contextual morphology; compare to reconstruction | Teacher's “nuisance” removal can erase foam/turbidity that predicts observation error. Retain local shallow features and an explicit QC path; test thin boundary/dynamic probes. |
| Same-acquisition cross-sensor / cross-modal alignment | Transfer sensors and missing modalities with physical patch extents | Radar roughness and optical waterline differ; acquisitions at different tides/times need not be positive pairs. Use tightly matched times, stable-land anchors and modality-specific branches, or reject uncertain pairs. |
| Conditional temporal prediction | Irregular-time coastal evolution given elapsed time, tide and forcing; only after data supports it | Predicting a deterministic future encourages averaging over storm erosion/recovery; unconditional “same site = positive” destroys dynamics. Use conditional distributions, causal inputs and withheld event tests; not “all dates identical.” |

**Separate registration nuisance from change:** estimate alignment on stable inland features; never warp the waterline to make consecutive images agree. Known synthetic translation should change coordinates equivariantly; after undoing it, physical change should stay fixed. Real erosion, beach rotation, overwash, nourishment, sandbar change and proxy shifts must remain represented. Tide-related instantaneous movement is real for waterline extraction, even if a nuisance for a fixed-datum erosion target.

Avoid indiscriminate date pooling, annual composites and seasonal positives for event detection; also arbitrary multispectral colour jitter, crops deleting the shoreline, and location identifiers that substitute for morphology. Preserve elapsed time/year, native spectral response and effective GSD. Resampling 20/30m to 10m does not add spatial information. Rotation augmentation must rotate transects/wave direction consistently. Report missing-band and sensor-shift tests, not just one resampled grid.

## 5. Gated training and supervised posttraining (proposal; none run)

**Parent's input audit/result update (not executed independently by this unit):** only Narrabeen raw survey CSV is present; ground-truth pickles are gitignored and three other sites need external archives. Stage 0 is now **one-site temporal feasibility, not leave-site-out**: fit through 2009 (**138 rows**), calibration 2010–2016 (**194**), test 2017–2019 (**329**). Parent reports exploratory HGB quantiles at about **8.4m retained RMSE**, versus a reported published baseline **6.74m** and simple disagreement **6.49m at 70% scenes**; metric supports must be checked before declaring superiority. Endpoint intervals reach **95% empirical coverage but about 36m width**, with only **1/138 pairs** producing a claim. This is evidence for conservative unresolved-change reporting, **not proof that a backbone is needed**. CASSIE sample timestamps differ by 12 hours; Deltares/UQMAO composites are not per-image observations. Audit these mismatches before attributing residuals to image difficulty. Row counts are not independent-scene/site counts.

1. **Stage 0 — metadata/label feasibility first.** Keep the present one-site time-block test explicitly local; obtain independently licensed external survey archives before a true leave-site-out study. Build survey residuals with explicit target support; block neighbouring transects/dates/events together. Compare no-learned model, per-method robust baseline, physical noise floor, metadata-only linear/GAM/tree/quantile model and simple disagreement QC. Negative HGB results call for label/matching/physics audits, not a larger model. A successful metadata pilot justifies a useful demo, not necessarily image pretraining.
2. **Stage 1 — controlled imagery pilot, only with later approval and licences.** Obtain a small legal sample and independent quality/proxy annotations. Evaluate frozen compatible EO encoders with the same small head, then LoRA/adapters and full fine-tuning. Use train/validation/calibration/test partitions fixed before model selection. Image gain must survive held-out sites and metadata-only controls; otherwise keep the cheaper system.
3. **Stage 2 — coastal domain-adaptive pretraining.** Continue one shortlisted existing encoder on legally usable, diverse coastal imagery, excluding entire evaluation regions and buffered neighbours even without labels. Balanced morphology/tidal-regime/sensor sampling; provenance deduplication and general-EO replay to measure forgetting. Start with its native SSL objective; test one alternative objective at a time. No random-init large-scale run until compatible pretrained models demonstrably fail and data/budget are adequate.
4. **Stage 3 — supervised multi-task posttraining.** Fit extraction, proxy, QC and survey-error heads on independent labels; quantile/pinball or likelihood loss for signed error, explicit exceedance classification, boundary-aware loss where appropriate. Treat weak teacher outputs as separately tagged supervision. Balance sparse survey labels without inventing dense truth. Separate study of temporal change from forecasts; forecasts cannot use future tides/waves unavailable at the stated issue time. No RLHF.
5. **Stage 4 — held-out calibration, then locked test.** Freeze all model/head choices before calibrating residual or conformalised-quantile intervals. Calibration and final evaluation must be disjoint in site/time/event support; do not tune abstention after seeing final outcomes. Use joint pair-residual calibration for change, or conservative simultaneous endpoint intervals; naive independent-error propagation is unjustified.

**Critical calibration limit:** ordinary split conformal gives marginal coverage under exchangeability, not universal per-beach coverage ([Angelopoulos & Bates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511)). New geology/tidal regimes and correlated transects violate simple IID assumptions. Four sites are too few for a finite **90% or 95% new-site group-conformal quantile**: with only three calibration sites, the order statistic ceil((n+1)(1-alpha)) exceeds n for both levels. At least 9 or 19 calibration units respectively are needed even to avoid this finite-sample obstruction; that alone does not establish exchangeability. Thousands of correlated rows do not become thousands of independent sites.

For Stage 0 report **empirical leave-site-out coverage and failures**, and separately same-site blocked-time calibration. Block/group conformal is a future candidate once enough independent units exist; weighted conformal needs justified shift assumptions. A no-survey public user cannot be promised calibrated site-specific intervals. Show transfer status, data support and abstain instead of silently labelling an interval “95% guaranteed.”

## 6. Matched-budget comparison and exact win/kill gates (proposal)

**Ablation ladder:** metadata-only; each frozen encoder + identical head; same encoder LoRA; full fine-tune; native coastal continuation + same posttraining; local/latent versus reconstructive continuation; physics on/off; image on/off; temporal on/off; location on/off; small random-init architecture control. Random-init large foundation training is not the first control.

Match splits, labelled counts, crop physical extent, modalities, target support, seeds (at least three), augmentation and hyperparameter-search budget. Match training FLOPs or accelerator-hours where possible, reporting both; “same epochs” alone is unfair across model sizes. Report upstream pretraining cost as sunk external compute, plus ours separately. Use common-band optical track and separately a native-input/multimodal track—do not give one encoder more sensors and call the gain architectural. Precompute frozen features only after approval. Report latency/memory and low-label curves (1%, 10%, 100% **independent groups**, not duplicate pixels).

**Preregister thresholds before outcome inspection**; these are proposed engineering gates, not published universal standards:

- **Learnability prerequisite (before any backbone training):** after timestamp/composite/datum/support audits, obtain a later-approved, licensed image subset stratified across high/low residual, sensor, tide and real-change events, with independent reason/proxy labels blinded to model scores. Test whether human-visible cloud/foam/registration/proxy problems explain out-of-time residuals beyond metadata and disagreement. With no reproducible visual-error association or too few independent labels, do not expand imagery or claim an encoder can fix the gap. Human QC labels do not become survey residual truth; an association motivates a controlled image test, not a causal conclusion.
- **Imagery gate:** at 80% retained observation coverage, ≥10% lower held-out-site MAE than the best metadata/physics baseline, with positive improvement on ≥3/4 pilot sites and none >10% worse. Also report all-row error and coverage across every tested threshold; block-bootstrap within site, and acknowledge only four independent sites. Failure kills imagery expansion for the demo, not coastal research generally.
- **SSL gate:** continued pretraining must beat the same non-continued encoder under matched posttraining budget by ≥10% on the preregistered primary error/risk metric, with no >5% relative degradation on change-event recall or a held-out general-EO probe. Otherwise keep the pretrained checkpoint; kill the extra SSL stage. Do not accept prettier segmentation as a substitute.
- **Safety/abstention gate:** target ≥90% empirical interval coverage on each sufficiently sampled locked site/regime; median width must be ≤the minimum change size the application wants to report. If coverage fails or width is too large, mark change unresolved; do not narrow it for presentation. At α=0.1, report uncertainty on measured coverage, not a new-site guarantee.
- **Dynamics gate:** on independently surveyed real events exceeding 2×the survey/matching noise scale, ≥90% correct displacement sign; no false significant movement on known no-change controls after stable-land alignment. Test synthetic shift/missing bands/cloud and real tide/storm subsets separately. Any high-confidence fabricated change blocks deployment of automatic change statements.
- **Foundation naming gate:** expand beyond these four sandy beaches and demonstrate one reusable encoder across **≥3 independently labelled task families** (not three heads on the same pseudo-label), **≥3 held-out coastal regimes/regions** and **≥2 sensors**, with reproducible low-label improvement versus frozen/adapted EO baselines and a clear rights audit. These are proposed minimum study criteria, not a formal definition. Until then say “coastal-adapted EO encoder” or “shoreline reliability model.”

The four-site gates are **future admission tests**, not measurements available from the current one-site pilot. Endpoint 95% marginal coverage also does not automatically give 95% simultaneous change-pair coverage, especially after selecting retained scenes; evaluate the final selective pair decision directly.

## 7. Implication for a useful public HF demo

Start with surveyed benchmark timelines: observed estimates, field comparisons, method disagreement, physical limitations, and “change unresolved” when noise dominates. Include a hard no-survey/OOD flag. Use frozen published evidence and clearly distinguish retrospective survey evaluation from predictions at an unsurveyed user location. Do not expose borrowed imagery, annotations, code or model assets until their separate rights are checked; **exclude SWED and repackages** under the governing UKHO restriction. No deployment is authorised in this unit.

**Bottom line:** the pretraining objective should preserve coastal boundaries and dynamic states while modelling acquisition error separately. The evidence favours testing continued pretraining of an existing encoder, not assuming a new architecture, JEPA, temporal invariance or scale will solve measurement error. The public product can be useful before—or without—any new foundation model.
