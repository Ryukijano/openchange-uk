 # Red-team: clean-JEPA (LeJEPA) scaling study on Earth-observation data

Scope: I read CLEAN_JEPA_PLAN.md in full. Sources are abstracts, arXiv HTML, OpenReview, HF and GitHub pages. I trained nothing. Compute figures are my own arithmetic from cited numbers, with the assumptions stated.

## 0. Summary

- No paper does a LeJEPA/SIGReg scaling study on EO, and none tests LeJEPA's "loss predicts downstream" claim on EO. That gap is real but narrow: T-JEPA, Earth-JEPA, a LeWorldModel adaptation to Sentinel-2 and HQ-JEPA already cover most T1/T2 mechanics at small scale.
- The strongest question available is a contradiction in the literature. LeJEPA reports Spearman ρ ≈ 0.85 (up to 0.99 after a λ-rescaling) between training loss and linear-probe accuracy. TESSERA v2 reports r = −0.18 across 395 EO runs with Barlow Twins. Nobody has tested which holds on EO, or why. Make that the paper, with T1/T2 as ablations.
- The FLOPs fit easily. The plan's grid costs about 12% of 60k GPU-hours without token dropping. The single biggest cost is **reading pixels**: ~1 MB per sample, and the bytes per sample do not shrink when SIGReg-style token dropping shrinks FLOPs.
- Corrections to the plan:
  - §1 says the loss claim rests on "one plot, ImageNet-1k, ViT-B". LeJEPA reports it across several datasets and models (Figs 1, 10, 11, 19), so state the weakness precisely: within-family hyperparameter sweeps, linear probe only.
  - §7.4: scratch is 5 TiB, so the data has to live in $PROJECTDIR (200 TiB).
  - §2 says Major TOM S2 has 2.2M patches; the HF page lists 4,491,772 rows (UNVERIFIED which number counts unique patches).
- Verdict: **T1 NARROW** (to a probe/ablation), **T2 NARROW** (temporal views as positives plus linear latent forecasting as a probe; no "world model" claim), **T3 KEEP** (as the out-of-distribution evaluation axis, not a contribution on its own).

## 2. Claim audit

**What LeJEPA claims** (arXiv:2511.08544, §6.2, Fig. 1/10/11/19; https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08544): the training loss has "Spearman correlation … of about 85%" with frozen linear-probe accuracy. Dividing the loss by λ^α raises this to "99% for α = 0.4". The points span "learning rate, weight decay, number of epochs, λ". The paper calls this "the first practical loss for model selection without supervised probing".

Weaknesses: the correlation is across hyperparameter settings within one dataset/architecture family, against linear-probe classification. It is not shown across a size × data grid, for dense tasks, or for transfer. α is fitted on the points it is reported on, so model selection would need α fixed in advance.

**Replications and contradictions since Nov 2025.** I found no paper that independently replicates or refutes the specific loss→downstream correlation. The related evidence:
- **Against, different objective, same domain:** TESSERA v2 (arXiv:2607.03949) finds converged Barlow Twins loss vs. a 15-task composite has Pearson r = −0.18 and Spearman ρ = −0.16 over 395 runs. Selecting by loss needs "2–5×" more compute to match downstream-driven selection. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03949
- **Weakening, LeJEPA itself:** *Self-Supervised Pretraining of Molecular Graph Encoders with LeJEPA* (arXiv:2609.04261) reports that "pretraining improves learned representations but does not robustly improve finetuning". The probe gain did not transfer to fine-tuning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04261
- **Weakening, SIGReg for downstream use:** VIScore (arXiv:2608.11174) finds SIGReg, "despite being beneficial in SSL, does not help the planning". PowerJEPA (ICLR 2027 submission) finds LeJEPA extended to text "yields limited downstream performance" and that "larger effective dimension does not consistently improve performance". https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11174 , https://openreview.net/forum?id=DvKUPN9lm6
- **Theory limits:** *Representation Selection in Capacity-Limited LeJEPA* (ICLR 2027 submission) shows that when embedding dimension < number of latents, slow latents and their nonlinear functions can crowd out fast ones, leaving predictable information outside the span (https://openreview.net/forum?id=UIZpTlKNek). *Shared Gaussianization* (arXiv:2610.10299) characterises what a SIGReg-type test certifies and what it misses. *When Does LeJEPA Learn a World Model?* (arXiv:2605.26379) proves linear identifiability only for stationary, additive-noise latent transitions with Gaussian latents.

**Does LeVJEPA report scaling curves?** No. LeVJEPA (arXiv:2608.27395) compares ViT-S/B/L at matched epochs and at matched FLOPs: ViT-B uses 4.8 vs 36.4 ExaFLOPs for V-JEPA 2. It runs one data-scaling experiment: ViT-L, 100 epochs on a larger corpus, 69.5% IN-1K, +9.5 points. The discussion says behaviour "at the model and data scales of recent video foundation models … remain[s] to be characterized." I found no power-law fit and no loss-vs-downstream analysis (searched the HTML for "correlat", "power law", "scaling"). https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27395

**Framing problem the plan should fix.** LeJEPA and LeVJEPA have no predictor network. The "prediction" term is invariance between view embeddings, plus SIGReg (LeVJEPA: "the architecture reduces to an encoder and a projector"). So a "clean JEPA" that translates S1→S2 or forecasts t→t+1 is either (a) treating those pairs as positive views, which is multimodal or temporal invariance and close to CROMA/Copernicus-FM-style alignment, or (b) adding a predictor, which is no longer the setting LeJEPA's guarantees cover. The plan needs to say which one it means.

## 3. Feasibility audit

**Hardware facts.** Isambard-AI GH200 = Grace CPU + one H100 with 96 GB HBM3 (TESSERA v2, which ran on 1,024 of them). TESSERA v2 uses a dense bf16 peak of ≈ 990 TFLOP/s. Storage (docs.isambard.ac.uk/user-documentation/information/system-storage/): $PROJECTDIR 200 TiB, $SCRATCHDIR 5 TiB, compute-node $LOCALDIR 48 GiB. Local staging of the corpus is therefore impossible, and all reads come from shared storage. I could not find Isambard-AI's aggregate Lustre bandwidth in the public docs (UNVERIFIED).

**Throughput estimate.**
- ViT-B/16 at 224² = 16.9 GMACs, ViT-L/16 = 59.7 GMACs (timm model cards: https://huggingface.co/timm/vit_base_patch16_224.augreg2_in21k_ft_in1k , https://huggingface.co/timm/vit_large_patch16_224.augreg_in21k_ft_in1k).
- Assumption: LeJEPA-style multi-crop of 2 global 224² + 6 local 96² views at patch 16 gives 2·196 + 6·36 = 608 tokens per sample. The plan doesn't fix a view recipe, so this count is my assumption.
- Assumption: 35–45% MFU, i.e. 350–450 TFLOP/s effective per GPU.
- Training FLOPs ≈ 6·N·tokens. ViT-B (86M): ~0.31 TFLOP/sample, giving ~1,100–1,400 samples/s and **~0.7–0.85M tokens/s per GPU**. ViT-L (304M): ~1.1 TFLOP/sample, giving ~320–410 samples/s and **~0.2–0.25M tokens/s per GPU**.
- These are planning estimates, not measurements (UNVERIFIED). Per-band-group tokenisation would add ~3–4× tokens. 20M models won't reach 35% MFU.
- Sanity check: I-JEPA trained ViT-H/14 "using 16 A100 GPUs in under 72 hours" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243).

**Grid cost.** Budget: 15,000 node-hours × 4 = 60,000 GPU-hours ≈ 8.6×10²² FLOPs at 400 TFLOP/s. The plan's grid: N ∈ {20M, 90M, 300M, 1B} × samples seen D ∈ {100M, 300M, 1B} (D values are my assumption), plus 3 seeds at the two smallest sizes. Σ6·N·608·D, +25% for attention, SIGReg and eval, comes to **≈1.05×10²² FLOPs ≈ 7,200 GPU-h ≈ 1,800 node-hours (~12% of budget), without token dropping**. Spectral grouping (×3–4 tokens) would take this to ~35–50%. The plan's 90–95% token dropping cuts encoder FLOPs ~10–20×. The largest cell (1B × 1B samples, no dropping) is ~3,200 GPU-h, ~100 h on 32 GPUs, so 5 chained 24 h jobs. The 1B row is ~70% of grid FLOPs.

**Data pipeline.** Copernicus-Pretrain is 20.7 TB for 18.7M images, CC-BY-4.0 (https://huggingface.co/datasets/wangyi111/Copernicus-Pretrain). That averages ≈ 1.1 MB per sample. Major TOM Core-S2L2A is 23 TB parquet, CC-BY-SA-4.0, 4.49M rows (https://huggingface.co/datasets/Major-TOM/Core-S2L2A). SSL4EO-S12 v1.1 is 2.3 TB, Zarr-in-tar webdataset, 246,144 locations × 4 timestamps (https://huggingface.co/datasets/embed2scale/SSL4EO-S12-v1.1). Note Major TOM's licence is ShareAlike.

ViT-B at ~1,200 samples/s/GPU needs **~1.3 GB/s per GPU, ~5 GB/s per node, ~170 GB/s for a 128-GPU job**, plus CPU decompression and band resampling. One pass over Copernicus-Pretrain is 20 TB. 1B samples seen is ~53 epochs, so ~1 PB read per large run. Token dropping makes this worse: FLOPs per sample fall but bytes per sample do not. Downloading ~60 TB takes ~17 h at a sustained 1 GB/s (ingress rate and HF throttling UNVERIFIED).

**Single biggest cost: data movement, not FLOPs.** For every model below ~300M, the loader sets the speed. If the shared filesystem gives a job 20 GB/s instead of 170 GB/s, ViT-B GPU-hours inflate ~8×. Mitigation: re-shard once into fixed-size, pre-cropped uint16 (or int8-quantised) webdataset shards at training resolution. Measure loader throughput on one node before the grid. Train small models with several runs per node sharing one read stream.

## 4. Reviewer simulation (ICLR/NeurIPS AC view)

**R1. "This is TESSERA v2 with a different loss, and T1/T2 are already done."** TESSERA v2 ran 395 EO runs on the same machine class and found loss useless for selection. HQ-JEPA/CR-JEPA do S1↔S2 with SIGReg. T-JEPA, Earth-JEPA and LeWM-EO do temporal latent prediction. *What answers it:* the plan's MAE and supervised baselines at one size are not enough. Run LeJEPA vs Barlow Twins vs MAE on identical data and eval at 3–4 shared grid cells. Report whether SIGReg loss predicts downstream where Barlow Twins loss does not, and explain the mechanism (e.g., SIGReg statistic vs effective rank vs probe). Fix α on a held-out split before computing correlations.

**R2. "Your 'downstream' is a choice, and the result depends on it."** The 152-paper audit (arXiv:2605.12678) found 46 cross-paper disagreements of ≥10 points. A correlation of loss with one composite score can flip with probe type, decoder or task mix. The molecular LeJEPA paper shows probe gains that do not carry to fine-tuning. *What answers it:* pre-register one harness (PANGAEA or GEO-Bench) with frozen linear probe + kNN + one fixed light decoder, and multiple seeds. Report correlations per task family (classification / segmentation / regression / change), with bootstrap CIs. Report fine-tuning on a subset. Release every checkpoint and the eval code.

**R3. "The scaling range cannot support a scaling law."** Three data points per axis underdetermine a fit. RS models look data-limited (2512.23903) and over-parameterised at small scale (2601.22841). 18.7M images contain heavy spatial and temporal redundancy, so "data scale" partly measures repetition. *What answers it:* build the data axis from unique locations (deduplicate by grid cell) with epochs as a separate axis. Use ≥4–5 points on two iso-FLOP slices. Fit with bootstrapped CIs and test extrapolation: predict the largest run's downstream score before training it, as RoboJEPA (arXiv:2610.10515) does for imagination error. If downstream is flat over 20M–1B, report that as the finding.

## 5. Verdicts

**T1, cross-sensor latent translation (SAR→optical, S2→S3/S5P): NARROW.** As a headline it collides with GeoSET and TerraMind (generative translation), HQ-JEPA, CR-JEPA and X-JEPA (S1↔S2 latent prediction with SIGReg), and Copernicus-FM/THOR (unified S1/S2/S3). With predictor-free LeJEPA it reduces to multimodal invariance. S5P is a coarse atmospheric product whose ground footprint does not match S2 tiles, so "translation" there is ill-posed (UNVERIFIED for Copernicus-Pretrain's exact S5P grid). Keep one thing: paired S1/S2 as positive views (an ablation axis), and a cheap probe of whether S1-embedding→S2-embedding linear predictability rises with scale and tracks downstream SAR-only performance (cloud-covered scenes). Drop S3/S5P.

**T2, temporal latent forecasting: NARROW.** T-JEPA (4 days old), Earth-JEPA and the LeWM-EO paper already cover the mechanism. *One-Step Next-Latent Prediction Is Not a World Model* (arXiv:2609.36227) makes it unsafe to claim an "EO world model" from one-step latent prediction. Temporal depth in open corpora is shallow: SSL4EO-S12 v1.1 has 4 seasonal timestamps per location. I did not verify Copernicus-Pretrain's or Major TOM's per-location time depth (UNVERIFIED). What remains strong is that 2605.26379's identifiability guarantee is about stationary, additive-noise temporal transitions. So using temporal pairs as LeJEPA positives is the theoretically motivated design, and it is testable: compare temporal positives vs augmentation-only positives, and ask whether linear k-step embedding forecasting error falls with scale and predicts change-detection performance. Don't call it a world model.

**T3, geographic/temporal transfer to held-out UK coasts: KEEP, as the out-of-distribution evaluation axis.** On its own it is a test set, not a top-venue contribution. As the OOD arm of the main question ("does the label-free loss predict *transfer*, not just in-distribution probes?") it is the cheapest original element and builds on existing work. Splits must stay as proposed by human review and be frozen before any model results, per the OpenChange-UK rules.

**Better-posed alternative: "When does a self-supervised loss predict downstream utility in EO?"** Resolve LeJEPA's ρ ≈ 0.85–0.99 against TESSERA v2's r = −0.18 with matched objectives (SIGReg vs Barlow Twins vs one masked-latent baseline) on one deduplicated Copernicus-Pretrain/SSL4EO corpus, over the plan's 4×3 grid (plus seeds). Evaluation: a pre-registered PANGAEA-style harness plus the UK held-out OOD split. Compare label-free proxies head to head: raw loss, λ-rescaled loss with α fixed in advance, SIGReg statistic, RankMe. Temporal and S1/S2 positives enter as two ablation rows. The headline is falsifiable either way, none of the three angles has to be novel, and it fits in ~12–50% of the budget.

    verdict_one_line: Gap open but narrow: refocus the paper on whether a self-supervised loss predicts downstream utility in EO (LeJEPA rho~0.85-0.99 vs TESSERA v2 r=-0.18). T1 NARROW, T2 NARROW, T3 KEEP as the OOD axis. FLOPs fit (~12-50% of 15k node-h); data I/O is the biggest cost.
