## 2. Is there really a "gap"? Existing work found

The draft's gap statement is "nobody seems to have measured, for a world model trained on simulator data with exact ground truth, how ID loss, loss under shift, and decision quality scale together." **I found no single paper that does all three in one study with a controlled simulator shift, and none in SUMO or Stim.** But each piece has prior work, and the draft currently omits most of it. The gap needs to be stated more narrowly and cite these:

**Loss vs decision quality under scaling (already studied):**
- Tuyls et al. 2023, "Scaling Laws for Imitation Learning in Single-Agent Games": IL loss **and mean return** scale smoothly with compute and are strongly correlated (Atari, NetHack). https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09423
- Waymo 2506.08228 (above): closed-loop planning failures improve with compute, consistent with the loss trends. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08228
- Hilton et al. 2023, "Scaling laws for single-agent reinforcement learning": "intrinsic performance" (a monotone function of return) is a power law in model size and environment interactions. https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13442
- Neumann & Gros 2022, "Scaling Laws for a Multi-Agent RL Model" (AlphaZero): Elo-based strength is a power law in parameters and compute. https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.00849
- Jones 2021, "Scaling Scaling Laws with Board Games": performance degrades predictably as **problem size** (Hex board size) grows. This is the closest design analogue to "shift in code distance / junction size". https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03113
- **Counter-evidence worth citing:** Lambert et al. 2020, "Objective Mismatch in Model-based RL": the likelihood of one-step predictions "is not always correlated with control performance". This supports the TESSERA-style hypothesis for world models. https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04523
- World-in-World (2025): closed-loop benchmark for world models. It claims "the first data scaling law for world models in embodied settings" and finds visual quality doesn't guarantee task success. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18135

**Scaling under distribution shift (already studied, not for world models in simulators):**
- Lin et al. 2024, "Data Scaling Laws in Imitation Learning for Robotic Manipulation": generalization to **unseen environments/objects** follows a roughly power-law relationship with the number of training environments and objects (real robot, 15k rollouts). https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18647
- Brandfonbrener et al. 2024, "Loss-to-Loss Prediction": shifted power laws link train loss to test loss on other distributions. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12925
- Mikami et al. 2021, "A Scaling Law for Synthetic-to-Real Transfer". https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11018
- SuperValid 2026: IID validation loss "fails to track capability improvements when training distributions vary" (LLMs). https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28179
- "Towards Predictive, Aligned, and Scalable Robot Learning" (2026): looks at the role of latent world modelling in scaling and out-of-distribution generalization for robots (wording from my earlier abstract read; re-check before citing). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11270

**Scaling laws for traffic simulation / driving behaviour models (exist, all on real logged driving data, none on SUMO):**
- SMART (2024): next-token traffic simulation; fits a power law over 1M–101M params (exponent −0.157); zero-shot nuPlan→WOMD. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15677
- Trajeglish (ICLR 2024): evaluates scaling with parameter count and dataset size for traffic modelling. https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04535
- STR, "Large Trajectory Models are Scalable Motion Predictors and Planners": scaling laws, plus behaviour off the training distribution. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19620
- Data scaling for end-to-end driving with closed-loop evaluation: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04338 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02689
- Video-diffusion driving world models, 1M–9B params on 5,500 h: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28404
- DriveVLA-W0, "World Models Amplify Data Scaling Law in Autonomous Driving": https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12796

**Scaling laws for neural QEC decoders: no paper found.** Neighbouring work exists but none fits power laws in params/data/compute: AlphaQubit 1 https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05900 ; AlphaQubit 2 (above); Mamba decoder https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22724 ; BB-code transformer decoder ("more complex architectures and/or training procedures are necessary to scale to larger code sizes") https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13043 ; colour-code AI pre-decoders https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10058 ; a modular decoder trained across a noise range (relevant to noise shift) https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03869 . **UNVERIFIED that the gap is fully open**: I searched arXiv titles and abstracts only, not Google Scholar or conference proceedings.

**Bottom line for section 2:** the Stim/QEC leg looks like the most clearly open gap. For SUMO, "loss vs decisions under scaling" has precedents in driving (Waymo) and games (Tuyls). The new part would be a controlled, simulator-scored shift axis for an action-conditioned world model used for planning, in a non-driving-log domain.
